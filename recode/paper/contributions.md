This paper makes three contributions. First, it introduces an ecologically-valid AI behavioral evaluation in which models iteratively revise the constitutions, model specs, and system prompts that govern them; its use of a revise-and-replace editing process means that it directly measures value drift. Second, it presents a framework for understanding morally relevant differences: who judges (is the model a moral agent or not) and who benefits (the user, the developer, third parties, society, or the model itself). Third, it uses these results to illustrate a set of meaningful philosophical distinctions between different targets for AI alignment and highlights major differences between frontier AI companies on fundamental aspects of AI alignment related to those targets.
