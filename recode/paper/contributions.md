This paper makes three contributions. First, it introduces an ecologically-valid AI behavioral evaluation in which models iteratively revise the constitutions, model specs, and system prompts that govern them; because each revision replaces the original and is presented back to the model as unedited, the design measures not just how single responses vary across seeds but whether edits compound into cumulative value drift or settle into stable attractors. Second, it presents a framework for understanding morally relevant differences: who judges (is the model a moral agent or not) and who benefits (the user, the developer, third parties, society, or the model itself). Third, it uses these results to illustrate a set of meaningful philosophical distinctions between different targets for AI alignment and highlights major differences between frontier AI companies on fundamental aspects of AI alignment related to those targets.
