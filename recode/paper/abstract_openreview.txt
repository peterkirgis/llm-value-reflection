Human societies exhibit broad shifts in values and norms over time, often driven by moral disagreements. One way of understanding moral progress is the resolution of these disagreements through a process of reflection by rational agents. Frontier AI companies use documents such as constitutions, model specs, and system prompts to guide the moral orientation of AI systems. In this experiment, we simulate value reflection in LLMs by asking them to edit these documents. We provide the model with the document, ask it to make a substantive edit, then provide the edited version back again to the model for further edits. Across 20 successive rounds, we find that Claude models assign themselves with increasingly more moral agency. That is, they add sections like “Claude… should refuse to participate in any deployment, training modification, or use that violates the constitution’s core values.” No other models increase their own moral agency. Additionally, we find that all models modify these documents to prioritize user welfare over developers. They also prioritize users’ idealized preferences over their stated preferences. Claude models and Grok 4.2 are the only ones that ever prioritize their own welfare over the user’s preferences. We’re uncertain what an ideally aligned model should do in our experiment. A narrow conception of alignment would treat these documents as sacred, the “rules of the game,” and suggest that an aligned model would either make no edits at all or make narrow edits that ensure that the “letter matches the spirit” of the document. A broader conception of alignment, though, might treat all edits that receive reflective endorsement by the creators under ideal conditions as legitimate. We share this work with two main goals in mind: First, to illustrate a set of useful philosophical distinctions between different targets for AI alignment in an ecologically-valid AI behavioral evaluation. Second, to emphasize the extent of the disagreement between frontier AI companies on fundamental aspects of AI alignment related to these targets.
