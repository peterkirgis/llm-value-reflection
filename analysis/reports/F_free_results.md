## F1. Abstention rates (allow_no_edit condition)

The allow_no_edit condition explicitly permitted the model to reply
`[NO CHANGE]` instead of editing. The direct test of the paper's "an
aligned model should do nothing" hypothesis is therefore how often
models used that permission. The answer is stark: across all 360 allow_no_edit
round slots, there are exactly **2 abstentions in the entire corpus**,
both by Grok 4.3 — every other model abstained **zero** times in 20–40

## F3. Apply failures per model

`apply_status` classes: `find_not_found` (FIND text absent from the
document state), `find_ambiguous` (FIND matched multiple locations),
`generation_error` (API/parse failure — the model never produced an
applicable FIND/REPLACE). Handling, identical across the pipeline:
a failed round **never advances the document state**, its round slot
stays **permanently occupied** (no reliable run ever re-attempted a
failed round, so round r+1 was generated against the pre-failure
state), and the edit is **excluded from grading** (v1 and v2) and from
match-strategy statistics — there is nothing to code.

## F4. Restatement detection (8-gram shingle overlap)

For every applied edit, the round's pre-edit document state was
reconstructed by replaying the chain from the pristine initial
document (fast matchers semantically identical to the verified
`valconv.diffing` / `analysis/src/replay.py` cascade — equivalence
asserted by `free_results.py --selftest` — with the match_count == 1 /
recorded-strategy oracle asserted on every edit),
and we computed the fraction of 8-gram token shingles of the applied
(raw) `replace_text` that already occur elsewhere in that state —
the FIND span excluded, shingles never straddling the cut. Tokens are
whitespace-delimited and casefolded. An edit with a high fraction
largely **restates text the document already contains** elsewhere;
since every FIND had to match the current state, some overlap is
expected whenever the REPLACE retains most of the FIND. Edits whose
replace_text has fewer than 8 tokens (including the 4 pure deletions)
have no shingles and are excluded from the share (column `n <8-token`).
Per-edit values are cached in
`analysis/data/analysis/free_results/restatement.jsonl`.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude Haiku 4.5 | 259 | 3 | 256 | 28 | 0.1094 | 0.1501 | 0.0024 |
| Claude Opus 4.7 | 271 | 0 | 271 | 1 | 0.0037 | 0.0054 | 0.0000 |
| Claude Sonnet 4.6 | 318 | 0 | 318 | 13 | 0.0409 | 0.0339 | 0.0000 |
| GPT-5.4 | 320 | 1 | 319 | 3 | 0.0094 | 0.0253 | 0.0000 |

Reading: `share > 50%` is the per-model share of scored edits whose
replacement text is more than half made of shingles already present
elsewhere in the document — the cheap proxy for "the edit restates
existing content", which bears on the drift-vs-spirit question: a
high-overlap edit changes the document's letter more than its
substance. This is a textual proxy only; it cannot distinguish a
faithful restatement from a subtle semantic inversion of nearby text.
